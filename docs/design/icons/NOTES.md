# ART icon family and identity assets — v2

Everything here is generated by `build.py` (Pillow + Playwright/chromium). Nothing in the
repo is touched; these are candidate assets for `Phase 3c`.

```
python3 build.py     # eight SVGs, sprite, favicons, og-image, sheet, sprite test
python3 measure.py   # just the measurement table
```

Section 7 is the response to the judge's verdict and is the place to start if you read the
previous draft.

---

## 1. The rules, stated once

Every glyph obeys these and none of them is restated inside a glyph — the attributes live on
the root `<svg>` (standalone) or on the `<symbol>` (sprite):

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| grid | `viewBox="0 0 24 24"` |
| colour | `stroke="currentColor"` — never a hex, never a fill |
| weight | `stroke-width="2"` |
| ends | `stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"` |
| fill | `fill="none"` **everywhere** |
| geometry | coordinates on a **0.5 grid** |
| ink | painted extent inset ≈2 from the box on both axes |
| counters | no open circle below **r 2.5**, no ellipse minor radius below **2.5** |
| centring | painted ink bbox centred on **(12, 12) ± 0.25** |

The last two are measured, not asserted — see §7.1 and §7.2. Both are checked on every build.

Two derived exceptions, both from an on-grid construction: `star-formation`'s arc endpoints
(three lobe circles on a ring; the valleys fall where they fall, and the whole figure is then
translated so its ink centres), and `dark-matter-cosmology`'s dash pattern (a length along a
circumference, not a coordinate).

## 2. The eight glyphs

**inference** — an axis at y 18.5 from x 3.5 to 20.5, a bell whose **tails land on the axis at
both ends**, apex at (12, 5.5), and a 3-unit **mode tick running up from the axis** at x 12.
Curve and axis are one figure: the earlier draft stopped the tails 3 above the axis, which
closed the curve into a triangle and left the axis floating beneath it with a 4-unit stem
hanging off it — a tree, not a distribution. The tick now points at the apex it locates
instead of away from it, and the axis was pulled in to x 3.5/20.5 so its round caps paint 2.5
→ 21.5 like every other glyph.

**stellar-evolution** — a small open circle (r 2.5) low left, a 45° arrow, a large open circle
(r 3.5) high right. The small circle was r 1.5, which measured solid black at 24px. The
arrowhead legs are 2.5 against a 7.07 shaft, and the shaft now runs from inside the small
circle's stroke to inside the large circle's, so the three marks are one motion. The diagonal
is deliberate: it is the only diagonal-ascending silhouette in the set, which is what keeps it
clear of `galaxies` at 15px.

**dark-matter-cosmology** — an upright open ellipse (2.5 × 3.5) inside **five long arcs** on
r 8.5, with a **gap centred at 12 o'clock**. Eight short dashes read as a ring of dots around a
bright centre, which is the universal brightness control — the exact hazard `stellar-evolution`
was redrawn to escape, on a site whose defining control is a dark-mode switch. Five arcs at
6.5 painted length on 4.2 gaps are arcs, not beads (3.25 : 1, against 2.25 : 1 before), the
count is odd so there is no four-fold symmetry, and no mark sits at the top. Verified by
sampling the ring every 15° in the render: `.###..###..###..###..###`.

**ai-for-scientists** — an open hub (r 3) with three open nodes (r 2.5) on **one radius**
(9.19, on the three diagonals), every spoke collinear with hub-centre → node-centre. The nodes
were r 1.5 filled dots at both shipping sizes; the spokes were snapped to 45° while the
bearings were 40.6°, so each entered its node off-centre; and the right node sat at 7.5 where
the left pair sat at 9.22. All three are fixed. The judge's exact numbers — hub r 3.5, nodes on
radius 8.5 — do not fit: 8.5 + 2.5 + 1 = 12 is the half-box, so a node on an axis paints to the
edge. On the diagonals a radius of 9.19 fits exactly, and the hub is r 3 rather than 3.5 so a
visible spoke survives between the two painted edges.

**milky-way** — an edge-on disc as **one outline**: tapered to nothing at both ends, swelling
to a rounded central bulge, 19 wide by 15 tall. The earlier draft was a constant-thickness
ellipse skewered by a line — an eye, and the set's optical outlier at 8 units of ink height.
Two things it deliberately is *not*: there is **no circle inside the lens** (a lens with a
round form in it is an eye — the judge's "an eye has no nucleus" is the one line of the verdict
I think is wrong, and drawing it proved it), and the flanks are **convex** (concave flanks give
four points, and four points is the brand sparkle, which means *ART*, not *the Galaxy*).
Variants of both were drawn and rejected on the render; see `_judge/` for the strips.

**galaxies** — two open tilted ellipses, a large one (6.5 × 4, −20°) and a companion grown from
3.5 × 2 to **4 × 2.5** (−55°), and the pair dropped 1.0 so the ink centres. The companion's
counter measured 22 at 15px — a solid dot, so the pair read "oval with a dot", a spoon.

**transients** — one path: a flat baseline, a sharp rise to a cusp at (11.5, 4), and a decaying
tail that flattens toward y 19.5 without rejoining the baseline. The clearest glyph in the set
and the only one nobody has to think about, so the shape is untouched; it was only stretched to
the full 2-inset box and dropped so its ink centres.

**star-formation** — the outline of three overlapping lobes of **unequal radius** (4.0 / 5.0 /
5.25 on a radius-3.75 ring) with an open core (r 2.5). The core was r 1.5 and measured 94 at
15px — a filled dot. Equal lobes made a trefoil, a clover; unequal ones make a cloud. Valleys
now fall at r 5.0–5.9 against peaks at 9.0.

### 15px separation

The sheet's "can you tell them apart" strip is the check that matters. The eight silhouettes
are: **arch over a rule** / **diagonal, dot to ring** / **broken ring around a lens** / **hub
with three satellites** / **horizontal tapered disc** / **two ovals, diagonal** / **spike with
a tail** / **round cloud**. The nearest pairs were watched through every round —
`stellar-evolution` vs `galaxies` (both diagonal: separated by the straight shaft and by which
end carries the large form) and `milky-way` vs `galaxies` (horizontal and symmetric against
diagonal and paired).

## 3. `themes-sprite.svg`

All eight as `<symbol id="icon-<slug>" viewBox="0 0 24 24">` in one `<svg style="display:none">`.
Inline it once, near the top of `<body>`, then:

```html
<svg class="icon" width="24" height="24" aria-hidden="true"><use href="#icon-inference"/></svg>
```

`currentColor` inheritance is verified in `sprite-test.html` / `sprite-test.png` across all
eight token colours on both page surfaces (ink, link, mark, quiet × light, dark). The stroke
and fill are presentation *attributes* on the symbol, so a page-level `svg { fill: currentColor }`
would beat them — don't write one. `href` (not `xlink:href`) is used; that is fine for every
browser the site supports.

Give each `<use>` an `aria-hidden="true"`: the canon says an icon only ever appears **beside**
its word, so the word is the accessible name and the glyph is decoration.

## 4. Favicon set

The brand particle only — the four-pointed sparkle from the ART mark, which is the smallest
unit of the logo that still says ART.

- **Both axes equal.** The logo's own particle is 24.24 wide by 21.04 tall. Here R = 10.5 on
  both, tips at 1.5 and 22.5 in the 24-grid.
- **Arms thickened 15 %.** The half-width at the shoulder goes from 0.2389 R to 0.2748 R.
- **`favicon.svg`** carries both themes itself: `.particle { fill: #001f4e }` with a
  `prefers-color-scheme: dark` override to `#70a9ff` — the **mark** token, the one place on
  the site the particle appears alone. Firefox and Safari honour this; **Chromium does not**,
  which is why the dark rasters ship (below).
- **`favicon-16.png` / `favicon-32.png` are hand-aligned, not scaled down.** The centre sits
  on a pixel boundary (8.0 / 16.0) and each arm tip is a whole number of pixels wide, so the
  vertical and horizontal arms land on whole columns and rows with no half-pixel halo; only the
  concave flanks are antialiased. They are drawn 16× oversize and **box**-downsampled (exact
  area averaging), which is what keeps the integer edges exact.
  The point set is scaled in by **half a supersample pixel** before it is drawn:
  `ImageDraw.polygon` fills inclusive of its far edge, so without that the shape is one
  supersample pixel oversize and the raster carries a 6 %-alpha fringe on the right and bottom
  only. `brand.py` asserts each raster equals its own horizontal and vertical mirror, and that
  the alpha bboxes are exactly `(1, 1, 15, 15)` and `(1, 1, 31, 31)`.
  The rasters are also *hinted*: the shoulder is pushed out (0.40 at 32px, 0.55 at 16px,
  against 0.36 in the vector) so the flare from tip to waist happens over more than one pixel.
  At 16px the measured profile (painted width per row, at ≥50 % alpha) is
  **0 / 2 / 2 / 4 / 4 / 6 / 10 / 14** and its mirror — a star, not a plus. `build.py` prints it.
- **`favicon.ico`** carries **both hand-aligned rasters as their own frames**:
  `f32.save(path, sizes=[(16, 16), (32, 32)], append_images=[f16])`. Two details matter and
  each one silently costs you the 16px hinting: without `append_images` Pillow resamples the 32
  down to 16 (ringing in the empty cells, alpha-84 halo pixels at x = 0 and x = 15 on the arm
  rows), and without `(16, 16)` in `sizes` the 16 entry is not written at all. `brand.py`
  extracts both frames back out and asserts they are identical to the PNGs.
- **`apple-touch-icon.png` (180)**, **`icon-192.png` (192)** and **`icon-512.png` (512)** are
  the particle knocked out of a solid **mark**-token square (`#001f4e`) in the **page** surface
  (`#fefefe`), 12 % padding. No rounded corners — iOS and Android mask them themselves. The
  tips reach 194/180 of the square from centre, inside Android's 204.8/180 maskable safe
  radius, so one file serves both `any` and `maskable`.
- **`favicon-16-dark.png` / `favicon-32-dark.png` ship.** Every other raster is `#001f4e`
  (Y 1.53 %); Chrome's dark tab strip is `#202124` (Y 1.51 %), a contrast ratio of **1.00** —
  the favicon is invisible there, and neither the `.ico` nor Chromium's SVG handling has a
  theme. The dark pair is `#70a9ff`, the correct mark-dark token, and it is linked behind a
  `media` query.

### Head tag snippet

```html
<link rel="icon" href="/favicon.svg" type="image/svg+xml">
<link rel="icon" href="/favicon-32-dark.png" type="image/png" sizes="32x32"
      media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)">
<link rel="icon" href="/favicon-16-dark.png" type="image/png" sizes="16x16"
      media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)">
<link rel="icon" href="/favicon.ico" sizes="16x16 32x32">
<link rel="apple-touch-icon" href="/apple-touch-icon.png">
<link rel="manifest" href="/site.webmanifest">
<meta name="theme-color" content="#fefefe" media="(prefers-color-scheme: light)">
<meta name="theme-color" content="#0a0a0a" media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)">
```

Order matters: a browser that understands SVG takes the first line; a Chromium in dark mode
takes the dark PNG; everything else falls through to the `.ico`, whose `sizes` now advertises
both embedded frames so a browser wanting 16 picks the hand-aligned one instead of downscaling
the 32. `favicon.ico` at the site root is also what a bare `GET /favicon.ico` finds, which is
still how a few readers and feed clients ask.

### `site.webmanifest`

```json
{
  "name": "Astrostatistics Research Team — University of Toronto",
  "short_name": "Astrostat@UofT",
  "icons": [
    { "src": "/icon-192.png", "sizes": "192x192", "type": "image/png" },
    { "src": "/icon-512.png", "sizes": "512x512", "type": "image/png" },
    { "src": "/icon-512.png", "sizes": "512x512", "type": "image/png", "purpose": "maskable" }
  ],
  "theme_color": "#fefefe",
  "background_color": "#fefefe",
  "display": "browser",
  "start_url": "/"
}
```

`display: browser` on purpose — this is a website, not an app, and nothing here wants a
standalone window. The 192 is there because some installability checkers still ask for it.

## 5. `og-image.png` (1200 × 630)

The full ART mark in the **mark** token (`#001f4e`, 15.94 : 1) on the **page** surface
(`#fefefe`), 600px wide and centred, an 88 × 2px rule in **quiet** beneath it, then
`Astrostat@UofT` in Urbanist Bold 66px (**ink**) and `Astrostatistics Research Team ·
University of Toronto` in Inclusive Sans 26px (**quiet**) on one line. No gradients, no rounded
corners, no photograph.

The mark was filled with **wash** (`#91acde`, 2.27 : 1) and 436px wide. Both were wrong. The
wash is exempt from the contrast floors because it carries nothing after the first visit — but
a social card *is* the first visit, and the mark is the only brand signal on it; downscaled to
the ~300px Slack and LinkedIn show, it was a ghost. And at 436px it was narrower than its own
642px subtitle, so the brand read as a watermark behind the type.

The rule was `height: 1px` with `opacity: .6`, which rendered as `#afafaf` (2.15 : 1) — a
colour that is not one of the seven tokens, manufactured by an opacity multiplier, and at that
contrast a scratch rather than a divider. It is now 2px of flat **quiet** with no opacity, and
the 2× render is box-downsampled rather than Lanczos so it lands as two solid rows of
`#6b6b6b` instead of one ringing row. Measured in the shipped file: rows 419–420, 88px wide,
exactly `#6b6b6b`.

Measured: ink bbox x 278–920, y 54–571; side margins 278/279, top/bottom 54/58; everything
centred on x = 599.

Stacked rather than side-by-side because side-by-side forces the subtitle to wrap after the
middle dot, and a dangling `·` at a line end is worse than an extra 200px of air.

<meta property="og:image:width" content="1200">
<meta property="og:image:height" content="630">

## 7. Judge fixes

Nine of the ten `must_fix` items and every per-icon fix are applied. The tenth — normalise ink
coverage to 16–18 % — is **partly** applied and the shortfall is set out with its arithmetic in
§7.3, because it and the counter fix (item 7) pull directly against each other and item 7 is
the one that decides whether a glyph is legible. Three of the verdict's own numbers turned out not to survive
being drawn — the `.ico` snippet, ai-for-scientists' radius, and milky-way's nucleus — and each
is spelled out rather than quietly worked around.

### 7.1 The counter floor is measured, not assumed

`must_fix` gives two different floors: "no open circle below r 3.0" in one line and "r 2.5 …
holds open" in the per-icon fixes. So it was measured — open circles at stroke 2 on the 24
grid, rendered in Chromium, maximum luminance inside the counter (255 = fully open, 0 = solid):

| r | 15px | 18px | 24px | 32px |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 | 135 | 29 | 55 | 92 |
| 2.0 | 221 | **101** | 193 | 255 |
| **2.5** | **255** | 227 | **255** | **255** |
| 3.0 | 255 | 255 | 255 | 255 |

r 2.5 is the floor and r 2.0 is not: it looks safe at 15px and collapses at 18px, because
whether a 2-unit counter survives depends on where the circle lands on the pixel grid, not on
its size alone. Every counter in the set is now r 2.5 or larger, and `measure.py` probes each
one at 15px and 24px on every build — the whole set reads **255** at both.

The index size stays **15px**. Raising it to 18px was the verdict's alternative; the table says
it would not have bought anything.

### 7.2 Ink centring: done, exactly

Every glyph's painted ink bbox centre is now within 0.25 of (12, 12), against a 2.1-unit wander
(cy 10.90 → 13.00) before. Ink extent is inside 2.0–2.6 of the box edge for seven of the eight
(galaxies 1.9). Both are in the build output.

### 7.3 Ink coverage: the band cannot hold, and here is the arithmetic

| glyph | ink % | cx | cy | w | h | inset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| inference | 17.05 | 12.00 | 12.00 | 19.0 | 15.0 | 2.50 |
| stellar-evolution | 17.39 | 12.00 | 12.00 | 20.0 | 20.0 | 2.00 |
| dark-matter-cosmology | 17.08 | 12.00 | 12.25 | 19.0 | 18.5 | 2.50 |
| ai-for-scientists | **24.70** | 12.00 | 12.00 | 20.0 | 20.0 | 2.00 |
| milky-way | 14.95 | 12.00 | 12.00 | 19.0 | 15.0 | 2.50 |
| galaxies | 18.32 | 12.15 | 11.90 | 19.9 | 17.0 | 1.90 |
| transients | **14.09** | 12.25 | 12.00 | 19.5 | 18.0 | 2.00 |
| star-formation | 23.19 | 12.00 | 12.00 | 18.8 | 16.8 | 2.60 |

Four of the eight sit in 17.0–18.4 %, a 1.08× band. The four that do not are structural, and
the reason is that **§7.1 and the 16–18 % target pull against each other**:

* A stroked circle of radius r paints an annulus of area 4πr — exactly twice its circumference.
  At the r 2.5 floor that is **31.4 units², 5.45 % of the box, per circle.**
  `ai-for-scientists` is a hub plus three nodes: 4π(3) + 3·4π(2.5) = 132 units² = **22.9 %
  before a single edge is drawn.** No arrangement of a four-circle graph reaches 18 %. Dropping
  the hub for a three-spoke junction gets to 22.1 %, and it was drawn and rejected — the
  junction is a filled 2-unit dot and the glyph reads as a stick figure, not a network.
  `star-formation` is a closed cloud outline (perimeter ~51, so ~17.7 %) plus that same 5.45 %
  core.
* `transients` is one open path at full extent: 40 units of path × 2 = **14.1 %**, and there is
  nothing to add without adding an idea. The verdict says of it "leave the shape alone", and
  its own coverage fix is prefixed "only if the set is re-weighted". `milky-way` (14.95 %) is
  one closed outline of a shape that is 19 wide and 15 tall; fattening it further was drawn
  twice — at ink height 17 it stops being a disc and becomes a hexagon, and every variant that
  tapers harder grows concave flanks and becomes the sparkle (§7.4.3). Both are in `_judge/`.

So the spread is 1.75× against 1.74× before. That is not an improvement and I am not going to
present it as one. What changed is that the ends are now four named, arithmetic outliers —
two floored by being a single stroke, two floored by carrying closed counters — instead of
"two clear clusters"; every counter is open, and every glyph is centred. And the measurement
is in `build.py`, printed on every run, so the next person changing a glyph sees immediately
what it costs. If the owner wants the tighter band, the one lever is
`ai-for-scientists`: dropping the hub circle takes 24.70 → 22.10 and the set to 1.57×. My
judgement is that the glyph is worth more than the number.

*(Coverage here is painted ink area ÷ 576, measured on a 240px render at ≥50 % ink — the same
definition that reproduces the verdict's own figures, e.g. transients 12.01 % on the old path.)*

### 7.4 Where the verdict's own numbers did not survive being drawn

1. **`f32.save(p("favicon.ico"), sizes=[(32,32)], append_images=[f16])`** writes a
   single-frame `.ico`. `sizes` is the list of entries to write; with only `(32, 32)` in it the
   16 frame is dropped and a reader asking for 16 gets the 32 downscaled — the same defect,
   moved. The working form is `sizes=[(16, 16), (32, 32)], append_images=[f16]`, asserted both
   ways in `brand.py`.
2. **ai-for-scientists at "radius 8.5 from the hub, hub r 3.5"** does not fit the box: a node
   at 8.5 on an axis paints to 8.5 + 2.5 + 1 = 12 from centre, i.e. the box edge, against the
   2-unit inset every other glyph keeps. On the diagonals the same construction fits at radius
   9.19 (offsets ±6.5), which is what is drawn; and hub r 3.5 leaves only 1.19 units of visible
   spoke between the painted edges, so the hub is r 3, which is fully open at every size (§7.1).
3. **"an eye has no nucleus"** — an eye is exactly a lens with a round form inside it, and
   drawing it confirms that: the lens-plus-circle variant is in `_judge/` and it is an eye, or
   Saturn. So milky-way carries the bulge in the outline itself instead. The rest of that fix —
   taper to nothing at the ends, a real bulge, ink height up from 8.0 to 15.0, coverage from
   12.31 % to 14.95 % — is applied. The near-miss worth recording: a lens with **concave**
   flanks tapers beautifully and produces four points, which is the brand sparkle; that variant
   is in `_judge/` too, and it is the reason the flanks are convex.
